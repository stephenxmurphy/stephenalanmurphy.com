MAVinci · Mechanical Engineer

3-axis CNC Mill Design and Construction

When I joined MAVinci, I needed a way to prototype large foam parts quickly while I was designing their next generation drone. All the CNC mills available to purchase were expensive, had small working areas, and designed to cut metals. Given those challenges, I decided to design and build a 3-axis CNC router myself. It measured 6x10ft and it enabled the business to cut prototyping lead times down from 2 weeks to 2 days with significant cost savings.

I machined my designs myself on this CNC, assembled them, flight tested each prototype, and iterated on each design.

University of Victoria Centre for Aerospace Research

Pneumatic Launcher for <20 kg UAVs

I designed a fixed-wing drone launcher for aircraft up to 20 kg and takeoff speeds up to 20 m/s. After placing the drone on top of the launch platform and connecting it to the launch hook, the launcher pneumatically fires a piston out from the main barrel, propelling the aircraft to takeoff speed. I designed this system to be modular so that it could be disassembled and stored in a storage case and loaded into a vehicle for transport.

I also created a multilevel Simulink model to simulate the behavior of the pneumatic launcher.

University of Victoria

Master of Engineering with a focus on Zinc-air Battery Modeling and Renewable Technologies

For my master's project, I developed a thermo-electrochemical model of a novel zinc-air battery configuration to evaluate the temperature and mass flow effects on cell potential and total power efficiency of the system. Zinc-air batteries are one of the primary candidates for large scale energy storage to address the misaligned timing between production and demand of renewable energies.

My model was written in Python using Jupyter Notebooks and was used to help understand the trade-offs of various geometrical and thermal parameters of the system.

University of Victoria AERO Team

Aircraft Design and Composite Construction of Drone

At UVic AERO, I designed fixed-wing aircraft to compete in an aerial surveying challenge at the Unmanned Systems Canada Student UAS Competition. First, I used XFLR5 to determine the wing loading, trim condition, and lift distribution of the aircraft. Once the low-fidelity XFLR5 model was complete, I designed the aircraft in SOLIDWORKS, then molded the aircraft out of composite materials using vacuum infusion, assembled the aircraft, and performed flight testing.

My team placed 3rd nationally while I was Aeronautical Lead of the team, and 2nd nationally while I was Team President.
